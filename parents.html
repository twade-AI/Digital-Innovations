Would you trust the voice note?

A fictional sports-club message sounds familiar and asks for an urgent payment. Pupils decide what they need to check before acting.

The teaching aim
Distinguish a convincing voice from a trustworthy instruction.
The tool’s contribution
The scenario examines how AI can imitate a voice and make a request sound credible.
The pupil’s decision
Which details need checking, and how can a known contact confirm the request?

Haileybury’s approach: what this means at home

Our four principles put human judgement, independent thinking, honesty and care at the centre of AI use. The same schoolwork rules apply when your child works at home.

  • Use the approved route. Gemini and Gemini Notebook through approved school accounts and arrangements; Canva and its school-approved AI features where the teacher permits; and Brisk Bots set up by teachers. Other AI platforms and personal AI accounts are not permitted for pupil schoolwork. Age restrictions, permitted features and teacher instructions apply.
  • Check the age and stage. Removes to Upper Sixth may use approved AI for general study support, subject to age restrictions and teacher direction. Lower School pupils may use AI only when explicitly instructed by the school.
  • Check this task’s instructions. For assessed work, the teacher chooses No AI, AI for planning or Full use of AI. Planning still requires the pupil’s own submitted work; full use requires demonstrated understanding and acknowledgement. GCSE, A-level and IB qualification rules take precedence. If it is unclear, ask before using AI.
  • Keep people safe. Leave personal information out of prompts and uploads. Check claims and sources. Never use AI to deceive, demean or create harmful synthetic content. Companions do not replace trusted adults or pastoral support.

The adult practicals in this course use your own eligible account and fictional materials. They do not give permission to use a personal account for your child’s schoolwork.
