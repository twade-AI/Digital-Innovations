Staff delivery outline · Prepared 11 November 2026

Try it, question it, improve it.

A proposed 40-minute parent session, with 30- and 45-minute options. Parents can take part alone or with their child. Use the existing course activities on screen; no account, video or written submission is required. Scheduling and invitations remain separate.

Before the session

Open lessons 1, 4, 6 and 8 and try the activities. Check the current School Portal guidance. For an online session, enable captions and read choices aloud. Offer large-print examples and keep agreed access support available. Parents can point, speak or select answers.

Use the supplied fictional examples throughout. For a live Gemini demonstration, use an approved adult demonstration account and close unrelated tabs and notifications. Parents who want to try their own eligible adult account can use another tab or split screen. No one needs to create an account during the session.

0–4 minutes · Start with a decision

Ask: “Your child has an answer that looks excellent. What would you ask next to find out whether they understand it?” Offer three responses: ask how long it took; ask them to explain a key step; ask whether they used AI.

Take one reason from the room. The AI-use question matters for school rules. Explaining a key step gives evidence about understanding. Introduce the two participation routes: use the on-screen examples or try an adult tool alongside them.

4–10 minutes · Predict, then test

Open the model lab in lesson 1. Show the original red-apple and red-tomato training examples. Ask what parents would want to test when green apples and yellow tomatoes appear. Take predictions before revealing the simulated results.

Ask: “Which examples would you add to training, and which would you keep separate to test the changed model?” Compare the results. Explain that the simulation illustrates a testing decision; it does not establish how a real system would behave. With older teenagers, ask what further checks would matter if the decisions affected people.

10–23 minutes · Choose, test and improve

Open the design lab in lesson 4. Choose the family game or Sixth Form event setting. Agree the brief: four people, twelve counters or budget tokens, ten minutes, and a useful decision for everyone.

Compare all three supplied drafts. Test one on the board and identify what fits or fails. Choose a revision, test it, then play the reasoning round: move one counter while keeping the total and every area involved. Ask what changed and what stayed the same.

Change the available resources to ten. Let parents design an allocation and name the decision that made it better. Explain that a model can propose options; the family decides what success means and whether the plan works.

Optional adult Gemini prompt: Suggest three designs for a ten-minute reasoning game for four people using twelve counters. Allow two minutes to explain, six to test and two to review. Everyone must take part and explain a decision. Use no additional equipment or elimination. State the target groups and turn-taking rules, show the total and identify assumptions. Use fictional details only.

Live users should test the response they actually receive. Ask for a specific revision based on a failure they found. The supplied drafts give everyone else the same decisions to practise.

23–30 minutes · Check what has been learned

Use the club-booking sequence in lesson 8. A booking needs both a form and payment. Ask about the first case, offer the short hint and then change the details. Let parents choose the next question that would reveal understanding.

Discuss why “Explain which condition is missing” tells us more than “Do you understand?” Keep normal reading, communication and access support in place. For older teenagers, examine the hidden assumption in the extension.

30–35 minutes · Connect the decision to school

Open the classroom comparison in lesson 6. Distinguish the verified Removes curriculum example from the illustrative teacher instructions. Ask why a teacher might permit AI-generated material for an exercise in checking it, then restrict generation when assessing a pupil’s independent explanation.

Invite parents to choose one useful question for the subject teacher. The purpose of the work, task instructions and qualification requirements determine permitted use; the parent course does not give blanket permission.

35–40 minutes · Questions and a next step

Invite a question about learning, one about school expectations and one about access. Parents can ask aloud or speak to the facilitator afterwards. Keep individual pastoral concerns out of the group exercise and use the school’s established support routes.

Finish with: “Choose one thing to try together: test an idea, check a claim or ask a better learning question. What will you look for to decide whether it helped?” Point to the three optional family conversation cards and written resources on the course page.

For a follow-up session, staff can retain an anonymous tally of topics: unclear task permissions, account obstacles, helpful explanations and claims that are difficult to check. Do not collect names, child details or chat histories for this exercise.

Adjust the length

30 minutes: use a three-minute opening, omit the six-minute classifier segment and shorten the creative challenge to ten minutes with supplied drafts. Keep the changed learning case and closing questions.

45 minutes: add a five-minute access clinic after the closing question. Those with working access can finish at forty minutes.

Access clinic

  • No account or a blocked tool: use the examples and refer the access question through the existing support channel. Do not change accounts to evade restrictions.
  • Uncertain account identity: ask the adult to check privately. Do not project account details or request credentials.
  • A phone makes switching awkward: keep the example on the shared screen, use one tab at a time or join another table’s discussion.
  • The interface differs: explain the purpose of the step. Use the prepared output if the required feature is unavailable.
  • An unhelpful response: return to one condition in the brief, identify the failure and request a targeted revision.
