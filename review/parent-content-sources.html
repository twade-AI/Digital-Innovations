Curation record · checked 10 November 2026 · Eight additional resources

More substance for the parent AI course

The best additions give parents something concrete to notice, practise and discuss. These sources extend the earlier comparator research. They were selected for clear connections to the fifteen approved lessons, current product details, pupil voice, accessibility and the distinction between completing work and learning.

Each entry separates what the source establishes from an original activity suggestion. This is a curation record, not a claim that every suggestion has been implemented. The final course uses on-screen choices and scenarios: earlier suggestions below for written evidence have been superseded by the no-writing design. All resources below are readable text or PDFs; none requires watching a video. No private family or school material is required.

Sources and precise teaching opportunities

Evidence-informed teaching guidance

1. EEF: metacognition and self-regulated learning

Read the guidance overview and resource collection · second edition, 13 November 2025.

Inspected: The substantive guidance overview and its linked practitioner-resource descriptions. It recommends explicit support for planning, monitoring and evaluating learning, embedded in particular subjects. This is school teaching guidance informed by research, not a trial of our parent course. The linked metacognitive-talk PDF did not produce readable extracted text, so detailed claims about that worksheet are excluded.

Insert in lessons 5 and 8: After explaining coaching, add a short worked conversation. A fictional pupil faces a geography explanation: the parent identifies the task, asks which class source would help, and pauses after the first attempt to ask what is still unclear. Then the parent steps back.

Original evidence task: Write one question for before, during and after the task. Explain what each question helps the pupil do. Feedback should favour purposeful questions over taking control or supplying the answer.

Qualitative pupil voice and practical guidance

2. Children's Commissioner: What I wish my parents or carers knew

Read the parent guide · December 2025.

Inspected: Method description, communication guidance and AI section. The guide draws on focus groups with 38 young people aged 13–18 and discussions with 19 Young Ambassadors/Youth SEND Panel members. It supports curiosity, adult role-modelling and explanations for boundaries. These are qualitative perspectives, not representative prevalence estimates. Its dated product-age table must not replace current provider guidance.

Insert in lessons 1, 13 or 15: A fictional parent has confidently shared a misleading local-news post. Their teenager notices. Give the parent a constructive response: acknowledge the mistake, explain how they will check it and invite the teenager to show what they noticed.

Original evidence task: Write a calm opening sentence, one genuine question and one boundary with a reason. A child does not have to participate or disclose private conversations to complete this activity.

Accessibility and learning-design guidance

3. CAST: access tools and graduated support

Accessible materials and assistive technologies · Graduated support for practice · UDL Guidelines 3.0, 2024.

Inspected: The explanatory text and practical recommendations on both pages. CAST distinguishes providing a tool from supporting its effective use, while retaining the challenge of the learning goal. It also recommends varied models, feedback and support that can gradually reduce. This is a design framework, not proof that any AI product improves SEND outcomes.

Insert in lessons 8 and 14: A fictional pupil can explain scientific ideas but finds a dense text difficult to decode. Compare read-aloud, chunked text and an AI-written finished answer. Ask which change removes an access barrier while leaving the reasoning with the pupil.

Original evidence task: Name the learning goal, the barrier and a support to try. Explain how understanding will be checked. Independent work can retain agreed assistive tools; it does not mean withdrawing accessibility support.

Primary survey research

4. eSafety: Talking to machines

Read the August 2026 report · Publisher summary.

Inspected: Definitions, survey method, findings and limitations. The study surveyed 1,950 Australian children aged 10–17 in February–March 2026. It found that personal and social use extends to products marketed as functional assistants. This is self-reported, non-probability online-panel research; it cannot establish UK prevalence, individual dependency or causal harm.

Insert in lesson 13: An original fictional revision conversation gradually moves into friendship worries. The parent notices that the young person describes the assistant as the only listener. Ask about the need the conversation is meeting, the tool's limits and available human support, rather than relying on its product category.

Original evidence task: Identify the change in purpose, write a supportive first response and choose a proportionate next step. Do not diagnose the fictional young person from one sentence or assume every personal interaction represents a crisis.

Practical regulator guidance

5. eSafety: deciding what to trust online

Read the critical-thinking guidance and cases.

Inspected: Explanations, verification tips and short cases about misinformation, dubious offers and urgent requests. The useful pattern is to interrupt pressure, investigate and check through another route. This is practical advice, not an evaluated intervention. The absence of wider reporting should be treated as uncertainty, not conclusive proof that a claim is false.

Insert in lessons 2 and 11: A fictional message says a school trip has changed venue and demands a same-day payment through an unfamiliar link. Provide three options: reply to the message, inspect its convincing formatting, or use the school's independently located contact route.

Original evidence task: Write the claim, the decision being requested, the independent verification route and what remains unknown. Model that an authentic-looking message still needs checking; visual oddities are not a reliable test of authenticity.

Current official product documentation

6. Google: importing sources into Gemini Notebook

Read the source-import help · checked 10 November 2026.

Inspected: Source types, import steps and limitations. The page currently documents automatic syncing for imported Drive files; Google-file comments and footnotes are omitted. Web imports do not include linked pages. Updating or deleting a source does not update previously generated Studio outputs. Feature availability can differ, and these details should be rechecked when the lesson is revised.

Insert in lessons 9 and 10: Show a supplied fictional event brief with a capacity correction. Ask the learner to inspect the actual imported source, then revisit a resource generated before the correction. Use pasted text or supplied sample outputs as the reproducible alternative.

Original evidence task: Record which version supports the answer, what information arrived in the import and which output needs regenerating and checking. This teaches a source-update problem, rather than assuming a citation or synchronised source makes every earlier output current.

Current official product documentation

7. Google: sources and related links in Gemini

Read the current Sources help · checked 10 November 2026.

Inspected: Google's explanation of the Sources button and related links. Links may appear within or below a response, but not all responses have them. The current page does not document the older green/orange Double-check workflow. That absence alone does not prove a feature has been removed; it does mean the course should not require that interface.

Insert in lessons 4 and 7: A response gives a factual claim and a related link. The parent opens the page and finds that it discusses the topic without supporting the precise claim. Give a second route when no Sources button appears: locate an appropriate source independently or label the answer unverified.

Original evidence task: Record a claim, the supporting passage if any, and whether it supports all, part or none of that claim. A related link is a starting point for checking, not a certificate of truth.

Preliminary primary research · author background

8. Liu and colleagues: course-integrated AI tutoring

Read IZA Discussion Paper 18989 · October 2026.

Inspected: Abstract, study setting, uptake, outcome table and authors' limitations. The university study reports adverse course-grade and platform-engagement results in its specified comparisons. It is preliminary research, with low tool uptake, other AI tools available and grades rather than an independently administered learning test. It should not become a blanket claim that AI tutors lower children's attainment.

Insert in lesson 8 as an optional evidence-reading example: Present an original fictional advert claiming that putting a chatbot inside a course guarantees learning. Ask what evidence would justify that claim, including who was studied, what was compared and whether understanding was tested independently.

Editorial recommendation: Keep headline effect sizes out of the main parent explanation. Use this as one reason to assess the learning process and outcomes, alongside the broader learning evidence already curated for the course. It is not evidence about Gemini specifically.

How to use this material responsibly

  • Keep teaching self-contained. Links provide optional depth and an audit trail. Supply the scenario, instructions, worked example and feedback within the lesson.
  • Label invented cases. The trip, event-capacity, revision and parent-message cases above are original fictional teaching suggestions, not incidents reported by the sources.
  • Retain uncertainty. A survey, a qualitative guide, a design framework and an experiment answer different questions. Do not present them as interchangeable proof.
  • Check access without assuming ability. Offer reading, typing, dictation or a private paper response where practical. The task should test judgement rather than speed or interface familiarity.
  • Use links and original wording. Public readability does not grant unrestricted republication. No licence to copy worksheets, illustrations, screenshots or full source text was assumed. Source titles and links are provided for attribution; examples should be newly authored. Check the exact licence before any later asset reuse.

Inspection limits: The EEF worksheet could not be read through text extraction, and a separate 2025 AI-tutoring paper was blocked by publisher/browser checks, so neither is represented as a fully inspected lesson or study here. No video resource, paid enrolment or private Drive content was used. Product help describes current documented behaviour; it is not a completed test across every account or device.
