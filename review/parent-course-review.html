AI at Home: A Parent’s Guide · Initial review · 10 November 2026 · See the subsequent review changes and current counts

Practical learning, without a worksheet.

The full fifteen-lesson course has been reviewed and improved. Parents read clear explanations, make decisions on screen and use feedback to try again. There are no written course assignments. Live Gemini practice remains optional, with complete supplied examples for parents who prefer to stay in the course.

15 lessons · 171 teaching slides · 21 inline interactive activities · 45 final checks. The teaching slides also contain eleven quizzes, worked examples and branching scenarios. Approximate total time is 5 hours 35 minutes, taken at the parent’s own pace.

How the course now works

  • Choose, select and order. Each lesson ends with three interactive checks. Feedback explains the reasoning; parents can change an answer and try again. Correct positions vary, and ordering uses keyboard-accessible controls.
  • Practice alongside the course. Copyable prompts and fictional source packs support Gemini and Gemini Notebook in another tab or window. Tool access and feature availability may vary; supplied examples provide a complete alternative.
  • School approach throughout. The November 2026 Parent Guide supplies the four principles, three assessment levels, year-group distinction and approved-account boundaries. Actual task instructions and qualification requirements take precedence.
  • A personal completion certificate. All fifteen specific lessons and their validated final checks must be complete. The parent then enters a name and downloads a landscape A4 PDF. The certificate uses the authentic Haileybury brandmark, primary colours and original Calluna Sans fonts. It is generated on the device.
  • Separate parent progress. Choices and completion save locally without changing pupil progress. Backup and restore support moving to another browser. Offline reading and certificate generation work after the required files have cached.

Verification and limits

Independent review checked the teaching, policy consistency, task instructions, question keys and feedback. Automated checks cover all rendered parent slides, completion and revocation, backup restoration, mobile layout, keyboard controls, enlarged text and offline use. Nine automated accessibility scans across light, dark and high-contrast lesson views reported no violations. These checks do not establish full accessibility conformance or measured learning impact.

Certificate checks cover incomplete and duplicate progress, answer validation, changing a completed answer, accented and long names, landscape dimensions, original embedded font data, local download and offline generation. Completion is a browser-local record, not a tamper-proof or accredited qualification.

A short parent usability pilot and a check of the live signed-in Google routes remain worthwhile before making claims about learning effectiveness. Product interfaces and school guidance need ongoing review. Names containing characters outside the supplied certificate font receive an explicit message without silently changing the spelling.
