AI at Home · Content-bank implementation · 11 November 2026

More to try, more to discuss.

The same 15 lessons now include six family activities, two interactive labs and an optional age-specific conversation card for each of the three units. There are 182 teaching slides, 24 inline questions and the existing 45 final checks. Estimated full-course time: 6 hours 20 minutes. Parents can work alone; family conversations are optional.

The six activities

ActivityImplementation
A · Recommendation feeds
Lesson 1		Compare two fictional screens, identify the changing pattern, consider possible signals and commercial interests, and choose a deliberate next step. The visible pattern is distinguished from claims about a real algorithm.
B · A useful, minimal prompt
Lesson 3		Sort a learning goal, deadline, school report, invented question, private message and medical detail. Discuss an ambiguous support note, then choose an assembled request that supplies useful context without personal records.
C · Choose, test and improve
Lesson 4		Agree success criteria, compare three supplied drafts and move twelve counters on screen. Test resources and participation, choose a revision, play a reasoning round and adapt to a ten-counter brief. A Sixth Form event variant and copyable adult Gemini prompt use the same checking process.
D · Evidence of learning
Lesson 8		Try the club-booking rule, use a hint, apply it to a changed case and choose the next teaching question. The older-teen extension investigates a hidden assumption. Agreed access support stays in place.
E · Test a model
Lesson 1		Inspect a fictional red-apple/red-tomato training record, predict results for new groups, reveal simulated predictions and split fresh examples between training and testing. Compare group results and consider a school application. Results are programmed illustrations, not real model measurements.
F · Understand the classroom boundary
Lesson 6		Use the verified Removes voice-note task as context. Compare explicitly illustrative teacher instructions for two learning aims, judge whether the same AI permission fits both, and choose a useful question for school. The example does not invent permission for the actual Removes lesson.

Make the course useful at home

Each unit ends with a conversation card offering questions for ages 11–13, 14–16 and 16–18, plus an optional activity that opens the relevant lesson. The existing school-teaching section remains visible on the landing page. Five optional written resources offer further activities and home–school discussion.

A separate 40-minute live parent-session outline includes supplied examples, optional Gemini practice, questions and an access clinic. It also offers 30- and 45-minute routes. No session has been booked and no videos are required.

Source use

All activities are original adaptations. Common Sense Media and Day of AI inform the feed, privacy and learning-conversation approaches. Vanderbilt’s public syllabus informs comparing options and iterative design. Experience AI informs the classifier and training/test distinction. AI for Education informs the home–school discussion. Public material was checked on 11 November 2026; no paid or account-restricted lesson content was copied.

The course’s written-resource links distinguish public reading from downloads that require an account. Haileybury’s November Parent Guide remains the parent-facing policy authority; task instructions and qualification rules take precedence.

Saved work and completion

The final three checks in every lesson are unchanged. Retained practice questions keep their original saved keys. Five retired questions have reserved keys that are never assigned to replacement questions; their stored records are left untouched. Six new questions start with new keys.

The two labs keep choices in memory for the current page session. They do not write progress or certificate state. The existing ten-minute starter still uses the same canonical museum explanations and awards no lesson credit. Pupil courses and their saved work remain separate.

Validation

The validation receipt records content, storage continuity, lab logic, browser, accessibility, keyboard, mobile and offline checks. All 197 rendered slides were checked at phone width. The new activities fit at 320px with 200% text; 15 selected activity/theme views had no automated accessibility violations. Both labs load and operate offline after the initial cache installation. These tests cover the supplied activity routes; they do not establish learning outcomes or replace a parent usability pilot and manual assistive-technology review.
