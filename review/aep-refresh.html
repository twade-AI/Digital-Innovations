AEP CONTENT & PLAYER REVIEW · 26 OCTOBER 2026

The existing AEP curriculum, refreshed for independent study. The course keeps its six units and 59 lessons, with clearer teaching, creative evidence tasks, current sources and a readable fullscreen player.

59 existing lessons59 visual explanations540 rendered slides8 refreshed reference guides

What changed

  • Reworked 57 decks and retained the two recently reviewed energy and regulation decks. No new AEP lessons were added.
  • Every lesson has a fictional or practical opening, an independent activity, a knowledge check, sources, an explanatory diagram and a creative evidence brief. The capstone retains its build, test, audit, presentation and defence sequence.
  • Corrected claims about models and tools, detection, copyright, employment, energy, privacy, companions and UCAS. Updated the quiz bank, 27 glossary entries, eight reference sheets and 23 recent timeline entries.
  • Kept optional videos with text alternatives. No lesson requires a paid account or a partner to complete its core task.
  • AEP now displays source lists and worked-answer reveals. Fullscreen starts in a readable scrolling view, with an optional fit-to-slide mode and a fallback when native fullscreen is unavailable.
  • Added an AEP evidence portfolio, including notes, reflections, downloads and print-to-PDF. Fixed the capstone export’s nine-slide limit and offline opening of new lesson links.
Existing work stays available. Lesson IDs, base slide counts, reflection positions and injected-lab order are retained. Main evidence artefacts remain attached to their original briefs. If a challenge or self-check changed, earlier responses and wording are preserved separately; old ticks do not answer new criteria.

Verification and practical limits

The automated browser sweep opens all 59 lessons and renders all 540 slides, including 25 interactive lab slides. It checks native fullscreen, browser fallback, keyboard navigation, answer reveals, saved work, legacy-response preservation, portfolio exports and offline deep links. Responsive checks cover 1440, 768 and 390 pixels in light, dark and high-contrast modes. Separate regression checks cover Removes, Passport and the project pathways.

This is an editorial and functional review, not a classroom trial or a full accessibility certification. Legal and product details remain date-sensitive. External videos require connectivity; their text alternatives and core tasks remain available offline. Labs are framed as simplified demonstrations, not evaluations of real products.

Recommended next steps

  1. Run a pupil trial across two age groups. Watch where learners hesitate, how long the tasks take and whether they can explain the idea afterwards without AI.
  2. Agree a small set of teacher exemplars showing developing, secure and ambitious evidence. Keep self-checks distinct from teacher assessment.
  3. Review the source-sensitive lessons once per term. Record changes to assessment guidance, product access and legal requirements rather than chasing every model launch.
  4. Test the complete journey with keyboard and screen-reader users, including the interactive labs, before claiming comprehensive accessibility.
