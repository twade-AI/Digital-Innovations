Your voice, when you’re ready.

The 15-lesson parent course is complete through written teaching and on-screen activities. These are suggestions for later recordings, not missing lessons or requirements for parents. No video placeholders or recording tasks appear in the learner journey.

Start with three clips: the welcome, supporting learning, and the closing family conversation. Your webinar already contains useful material for these. Review the original recording for clear audio and a complete explanation; slide headings below are locators, not verified timestamps.

1. Welcome: what this course is for

Lesson 1 · 90 seconds–2 minutes · Webinar edit or new piece to camera

Purpose: reassure parents that they do not have to become AI experts. They will practise asking useful questions, checking answers and helping their child make decisions.

Webinar starting points: “A balanced digital diet”, “Protect what matters” and “Four principles for AI”. If you use the chair analogy or Staretime / Scaffold / Stretch, retain Dr Fiona Aubrey-Smith’s credit. Credit Laura Knight for the adapted screen-purpose question.

Possible opening: “Think about the last time your child used a screen. What were they trying to do? Start there. The purpose matters.” End by inviting parents to think of one question they want the course to answer.

2. A convincing answer that needs checking

Lesson 2 · 2–3 minutes · New overhead or screen demonstration

Use the lesson’s fictional source and its flawed answer. Read one claim, point to the relevant source sentence and explain whether it is supported, contradicted or unknown. Show yourself changing your judgement. Pause before the final claim so the parent can try it. Do not present an AI detector as a way to prove authorship.

3. Haileybury’s approach in ordinary language

Lessons 3 and 6 · 3–4 minutes · New piece to camera with simple policy cards

Explain the four principles through one homework example. Show where a parent finds current school guidance, distinguish approved pupil school accounts from an adult’s practice account, and explain why task instructions and qualification rules take precedence. Check the published policy and current parent guide immediately before recording; avoid recording an unratified draft as settled policy.

End with a useful question for the teacher: “What help is permitted for this task, and what should my child keep or acknowledge?”

4. Give Gemini a job you can check

Lesson 4 · 3–5 minutes · Fresh screen recording

Use the course’s fictional planning brief in your own eligible adult account. Show a vague request, a clearer brief and one check of the output. Leave a mistake in the demonstration and explain the correction. Do not expose browser history, email, notifications, personal prompts or connected apps. Test the free route shortly before recording; keep any model labels incidental.

5. Don’t steal the struggle

Lessons 5 and 7 · 2–3 minutes · Webinar edit plus optional new worked example

Webinar starting point: “Keeping the thinking with the pupil”. Contrast “Write the answer for me” with “Give me a hint, then let me try”. Explain the question “Could you explain it without the tool?”

For a new version, model a calm parent response to “I’m stuck”. Show an initial attempt, one useful hint, then a changed question after the tool closes. Explain that suitable accessibility support stays available.

6. A citation is the beginning of a check

Lesson 9 · 4–5 minutes · Fresh Gemini Notebook screen recording

Use the fictional club notices supplied in the lesson. Add the sources, check what was imported, ask about the changed opening time and open a citation. Ask a question the sources cannot answer. Show how to distinguish a missing fact from an incorrect one. Keep the notebook restricted and use no real school or family records.

Use “Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)” initially. Recheck the interface, source-update behaviour and account restrictions before recording.

7. A revision resource is not yet revision

Lessons 8 and 10 · 3–4 minutes · Fresh screen recording with paper cutaway

Create a small quiz from the fictional source pack, check an answer and correct an unsupported item. Then close the resource and attempt a new question on paper. If a source has changed, explain why an older generated resource needs checking and potentially regenerating. The useful ending is the independent answer, not the creation animation.

8. When AI feels personal

Lesson 13 · 2–3 minutes · Webinar edit or new piece to camera

Webinar starting points: “When AI feels human” and “A chatbot is not a trusted adult”. Explain simulated empathy without dismissing a young person’s feelings. Model a curious first response and name a route to human support. Omit platform-traffic statistics unless freshly checked; visits and downloads do not establish pupil use. No live companion demonstration or personal family disclosure is needed.

9. One conversation. One change.

Lesson 15 · 90 seconds–2 minutes · Webinar closing extract

Webinar starting point: the slide of the same name. Invite parents to ask their child to show them something they value online, discuss what they want to protect, and choose one manageable change to review. Keep a parent studying alone included: they can consider which opening they would use and return to it later.

Recording and accessibility checklist

  • One learning purpose per clip. Keep the complete written explanation and task alongside it.
  • Supply edited captions and a transcript. Describe important visual changes aloud; avoid tiny interface text or instructions conveyed only by colour.
  • Use fictional data, hide notifications and review the whole recording before publication.
  • Keep pause points explicit: “Have a go before continuing.” Include the reason behind a decision, not only the answer.
  • Record product demonstrations close to publication, date them and review when interfaces or school guidance change.
  • Trim webinar extracts at natural sentence boundaries. Preserve attribution and remove unrelated speakers’ material unless its use has been agreed.
